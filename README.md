## About me

I am a doctoral researcher at the Research Institute Cyber Defence (CODE) at the Universität der Bundeswehr München, where I work in the AIML lab of Prof. Dr. Eirini Ntoutsi. My research is about the **adversarial robustness and trustworthiness of deep learning models**, in particular autoencoders, variational autoencoders and transformer-based vision-language models.

A common thread in my work is looking at models through their **linear-algebraic and geometric structure**: singular-value spectra, ill-conditioned layers, latent-space geometry and topology. I use these to find vulnerabilities that standard evaluations miss. For example, I have shown that near-zero singular values can hide a model's true vulnerability from gradient-based attacks, and that adversarial examples tend to exploit the information-attenuating (bottom) singular-vector subspaces of intermediate layers.

I came to machine learning from engineering. I worked as a mechanical engineer in industry, then did computational research on multiphase flows and fuel-cell modelling, and moved into topological data analysis and representation learning during my master's at TU Dresden.

## Research interests

- Adversarial robustness and robustness evaluation of deep neural networks
- Security of vision-language models and transformer architectures
- Autoencoders, VAEs and representation learning
- Spectral analysis of neural networks (singular-value structure, ill-conditioning)
- Topological and geometric data analysis of latent spaces
- Bias and fairness: disparities in robustness across data subgroups
